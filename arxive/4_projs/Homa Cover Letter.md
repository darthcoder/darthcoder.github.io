The indie video-game "That Dragon Cancer" was released, to critical acclaim in early 2016, when I was halfway through my M.Sc in Zoology. I am not a gamer by any stretch of the definition, but it showing up in my google alerts made me take a pause.

I had always been good at studies, and as a young Muslim woman born in the poorest state of India - namely Bihar, in a world that was on the cusp of rapidly accelerating unanticipated changes, academia - and by that not just the classes and the student life, had always felt like a safe refuge to me. I had always had an interest in literature, and my parents and grandparents had overcome significant challenges in raising me. Not only did they try to educate me equally as my twin brother, they practically encouraged my curiosity and intellectual explorations.

Just like the American dream, there is an Indian dream, though it is not often spoken of or discussed. You build on the foundation provided by your parents and hopefully your children can erect the next level of the structure. And, the one way to excellence, perhaps the only way forward was to do well, study really hard, have the best work ethic and once you have put in your best effort, have faith in God to carry you forward.

I left home after completing matriculation, i.e 10th grade (equivalent to Junior High as far as I can understand), enrolling at the famous Aligarh Muslim University(AMU) in ...... Founded in 1875 by one of the greatest Muslim reformers of his time, Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, be-knighted in 1888 under the rule of Queen Victoria herself. While the name does suggest and even in India, there is a perception of it being some sort of a theological seminary, in 2025 it was actually the 7th best public research university in India with a vibrant research program in the Life Sciences.

AMU has not just a long history but also a culture of free thinking, expression, reform, idealism, rationality, a sense of community, of belonging and even a distinct recognized identity. It might sound like a rather surprising simile, but isn't really that much of a stretch to state that I really felt like a muggle born witch who had just arrived at Hogwarts. 

By 2016, a different uncertainty had been slowly surging within me. I was working really hard and doing really well in my classes, and the Presidential Gold Medal (_"Valedictorian summa cum laude"_ in American terminology) was a tossup between me and my best friend. 

The question of what to do next was not about what to do next. Academia was my home, PhD was the logical conclusion. The question was not what next, but what did I want to pursue further. While Genetics was my specialization, Molecular Biology was my inspiration. Life springing out of nothing, guided by the blind watchmaker, and I wanted to understand as much of it as I could. And while on the surface bio molecules seem like really random agglomerates, they behave in aggregate like mechanisms as if made of levers and pulleys, even if somewhat Rube Goldbergish contraptions. 

And yet, That Dragon Cancer roamed, wild, untamed, ravaging and marauding at it pleased with barely a dint in its aggression. I had read about Cancer research and I had seen countless Bollywood masala movies, where Cancer was almost  always a lazy attempt to add some drama to the movie, often cured by either praying on a mat from Dusk till Dawn or ringing the Temple bells till your hands got bloodied (the dramatic music, curiously, the same in both cases). 

Cancer kills to the point of melodrama. And that is a fact. But that metaphor. That unique metaphor stayed with me, in my subconsciousness. While musing about these molecular machines, my thoughts would often stray to how cancer gets formed. Like a Jedi falling over to the Dark side, of gaining immortality at so terrible a price, becoming akin to an undead zombie, losing all function and regulation till all that remained of life, was just that it was alive. 

When I blinked again, it was Graduation Day. I was wearing my 5 gold medals, laughing and crying with the friends who had been with me for almost a decade, and knowing that this was the last time I would be seeing most of these people. Perhaps not actually knowing then, at least not consciously or in those words, but the changing world - the rise of Bioinformatics, _in silico_ research, Data Science, Deep Learning, MOOCs and so on. 

A life devoted to research had already long been a forgone conclusion. And not just that, while the Heroes and Brave Knights of yore had failed to slay the dragon, maybe it was the turn of the Damsel in Distress, forsaken atop the Ivory Tower, to shed her demureness and pick up the Silicon Scalpel in one hand, and a Pipette in the other, to excise this daemon wound from our collective soul, once and for all. 

And thus, with sheer passion and an indefatigable spirit, I undertook courses and research, via collaboration and on my own, while finishing my Doctorate, becoming a mother to a healthy, beautiful baby, despite unexpected complications not just in my pregnancy and motherhood, but in research, livelihood, family, life in general, rebuilding my strength, resting, recouping and getting prepared to embark on a new heroic quest, not just a researcher and scientist-in-training, but also a mother, a _Muslimah_, a daughter and a partner, and so much more.

And yet while the world had changed in my transition from a student to a researcher and became more about data, information, graphs and networks, I had first sought comfort in Wet Work. There was much yet to be done and learnt and mastered in the organic world of beating hearts(that there is no cancer of the cardiac muscles, a fact still etched in a corner of my mind for further investigation _one day_) and flowing breath, that multiplying matrices in silicon complexes can neither emulate nor simulate. 

To grow a tumor in a mouse, and to learn to identify it, to observe it metastasize with your eyes and not just feed it to a vision model or a neural network. To work with the chemicals - the reagents, enzymes and to truly comprehend the vital human component of cancer identification, management and mitigation - basically to know the mechanism of how cancer becomes Cancer. Even in this AI era of overpromise and under-delivery, has to have some value, at least until our Artificial Super Uber General Intelligence is itself inflicted with cancer.

And its not just that, while the crux of my research in my PhD was to study the role of Lupeol in management of Sorafenib induced drug toxicity, as anyone who has made the leap of faith from PhD to full time research knows, the research done in a Doctorate in practically an afterthought.

Beginning with the literature review and slowly getting my methodology for conducting a literature review, optimizing my methodology via stochastic gradient descent (honestly, its basically Computer Science slang for trial and error, but apparently calling it that pads your annual income with extra zeros) and reinforcement learning (the old masters called it learning from your mistakes, and the Wise simply call it Experience), till I managed to get a review in "Seminars in Cancer Biology". Starting from learning to use Adobe Illustrator to getting to make gorgeous vector illustrations just the way I envisioned it in my head, typesetting papers - research articles, meta-analyses, review papers and invited book chapters in high impact journals. Learning to communicate in the proper scientific cadence of my chosen field and mastering its unspoken yet conventional intricacies. Mastering the intellectual "editorial game" of the peer review process from both sides, as both the researcher writing the paper and the reviewer scrutinizing its claims and methodology. And to top it all of, by finishing in the top 100 of a national competition organized by the Government of India, Department of Science and Technology, to inculcate and promote scientific writing and public exposition amongst its young researchers.

And again, while the whole world might be a quantum soup that we are cosmically, chaotically, dynamically and undeterministically entangled in, suddenly, there was again a solid Earth below my two little feet.

And seemingly, as soon as the ground beneath me had just turned solid, the universe "throws another curveball", to use the American parlance. Covid, the public distrust of Science, the rise of pseudoscience, conspiracy theories especially with regards to vaccines and even, pasteurization - not only research, but good effective communication of research matter a lot more today, perhaps than they did ever. To not understand what pasteurization is, and why it is done, along with the rise of sloptubers, reaction videos, reels and the industrial strength anti Science content going viral cocurrently with the increasing capabilities of Artificial Intelligence is no happy coincidence.

There, alas, may be no point to defeating the dragon, lest human creativity and reasoning, indeed both the _logos_ and the _mythos_ perish in the attempt to slay the _Deimos_ incarnate.

Cancer has been a personal tragedy or an uphill battle in the personal lives of many of us. My youngest aunt suddenly developed Cancer in her late middle ages. As a researcher in Cancer, my family looks at me with a kind of forlorn hope that I dare not revoke, for I feet it too. Research had always been in some sense abstract, an edifice of facts, figures, code and numbers. Now it has materialized. That Dragon has just made it personal.

This is as far as I have come.
