# CAIIB BFM Module A: Unit 5
## Facilities for Exporters and Importers

---

## What Problem Does This Solve? (Why It Exists)

### The Cash Flow Trap (Exporter's Nightmare)

Imagine: Indian textile exporter gets export order for USD 100,000.

**The problem:**
- Exporter must buy raw cotton NOW (pays INR 80 lakhs today)
- Exporter manufactures fabric (takes 2 months, uses cash, labor, factory)
- Exporter ships fabric (month 3)
- Buyer receives fabric, inspects, then pays 30 days later (month 4)
- Exporter finally gets USD 100,000, converts to INR (month 4)

**But exporter is broke by month 2:** Paid for raw materials, labor, factory overhead, shipping — all upfront. No revenue yet.

**Solution:** Bank loans exporter money to cover production costs BEFORE shipment.

### Similar for Importers

Importer needs to buy machinery from Germany. 

**Problem:**
- Importer doesn't have USD today
- Supplier won't ship without payment upfront (or on LC)
- Importer is stuck

**Solution:** Bank gives importer short-term loan to pay supplier. Importer repays once goods arrive and are sold.

---

## What IS Export-Import Finance? (Grok Definition)

**Export-Import Finance = Short-term loans banks give to exporters/importers to bridge cash flow gap between order and payment.**

**Why banks do this:**
- Exporters/importers are creditworthy (have orders, collateral)
- Loan tenure is SHORT (90-180 days, not 5 years)
- Loan is SECURED (goods are collateral, or LC backs it)
- Risk is manageable

**Why exporters/importers use it:**
- Working capital is expensive (own cash is precious)
- Bank loan is cheaper than borrowing from money lender
- Enables them to take larger orders (without personal cash)

## The Two Types (Pre-Shipment & Post-Shipment)

### PRE-SHIPMENT FINANCE (PSC - Pre-Shipment Credit)

**What:** Loan given BEFORE goods are shipped  
**When:** Exporter gets order, buys raw materials, starts manufacturing  
**Amount:** Up to 90% of order value (or as per bank's policy)  
**Security:** Hypothecation of raw materials/work-in-progress + finished goods (when ready)  
**Tenor:** Typically 90-180 days (covers production + shipment time)  
**Interest Rate:** Higher (risky — goods don't exist yet, just promise)  
**Repayment:** Exporter repays when PSC is converted to post-shipment finance (see below)

**Real example:**
- Exporter gets order: Export 100 tons steel rods, USD 100K
- Bank gives PSC: 90% × USD 100K = USD 90K (in INR)
- Exporter buys steel scrap, melts, casts, ships
- Exporter repays PSC + interest from post-shipment credit (see next)

**Risk for bank:** If order is cancelled, exporter defaults, goods have no buyer → bank stuck with collateral (unsold raw steel rods).

### POST-SHIPMENT FINANCE (PSC - Post-Shipment Credit)

**What:** Loan given AFTER goods are shipped (documents submitted)  
**When:** Exporter submits Bill of Lading, Invoice, LC (if confirmed)  
**Amount:** Up to 90-95% of invoice value (depends on LC confirmation)  
**Security:** Bill of Lading (bank gets ownership title) + LC (if confirmed, bank is assured of payment)  
**Tenor:** Typically 90-180 days (covers shipping time + buyer's payment delay)  
**Interest Rate:** Lower (safer — goods already shipped, docs prove it)  
**Repayment:** When buyer pays LC, or when check arrives

**Real example:**
- Exporter ships steel rods, gets BL dated Nov 15
- Invoice says USD 100K, payment due Dec 15 (30 days after BL)
- Bank gives post-shipment finance: 95% × USD 100K = USD 95K (against BL + LC)
- Exporter gets INR equivalent immediately (doesn't wait 30 days for buyer payment)
- Buyer pays on Dec 15 → buyer's bank transfers USD 100K to exporter's bank → exporter's bank recovers post-shipment loan + interest

**Risk for bank:** Minimal. Goods exist (BL proof), buyer's bank has committed (LC), exporter has solid order book.

## Key Distinction (Exam Favorite)

| Feature | Pre-Shipment | Post-Shipment |
|---|---|---|
| When Given | Before shipment | After shipment |
| Security | Raw materials/WIP (future goods) | Bill of Lading (actual goods) |
| Risk Level | High (goods don't exist) | Low (goods shipped, docs prove it) |
| Interest Rate | Higher (5-6%) | Lower (3-4%) |
| Amount | 80-90% of order | 90-95% of invoice |
| Tenor | 90-180 days | 90-180 days |
| Typical Scenario | New order, production cost needed | Goods shipped, payment waiting |

## Export Incentive Schemes (Government Helping Exporters)

### Scheme 1: ADVANCE LICENSING

**What problem it solves:**
Exporter needs to import raw materials (e.g., copper wire from China) to manufacture export goods (cables for Germany). But importing incurs customs duty. Exporter's cost goes up.

**How it works:**
- Exporter gets license from customs: "You're importing X for export manufacturing"
- Importer is EXEMPTED from customs duty on raw materials
- Exporter manufactures, exports
- Net effect: Exporter's production cost is lower

**Example:**
- Normal: Import copper → 10% customs duty → higher cost
- With advance license: Import copper → 0% customs duty → lower cost
- Exporter can price competitively in Germany market

**Who benefits:** Exporter (lower input cost)  
**Who pays:** Government (foregoes duty revenue)

### Scheme 2: DUTY DRAWBACK

**What problem it solves:**
Exporter already imported raw materials, paid customs duty, manufactured goods, now exports. But that duty was paid on inputs that are now gone (exported). Seems unfair.

**How it works:**
- Exporter exports finished goods
- Exporter claims refund from customs: "I paid duty on materials used in this export"
- Customs calculates: (Duty paid on inputs / Cost of inputs) × Invoice value of exports
- Exporter gets refund

**Formula:**
```
Duty Drawback = (Total Duty Paid on Raw Materials / Total Cost of Raw Materials) × Export Invoice Value
```

**Example:**
- Exporter imported cotton at INR 100 with 10% duty = INR 10 duty paid
- Used cotton to make shirts, exported for INR 150
- Duty drawback claim: (10 / 100) × 150 = INR 15 (gets refunded)

**Who benefits:** Exporter (gets refund)  
**Who pays:** Government (refunds duty)

## Export Bills (Negotiation & Discounting)

### Export Bill Definition

**Export Bill** = Exporter's written demand for payment from importer (via importer's bank)

**Two types:**
- **Demand Bill** = Payable on sight (immediate payment)
- **Usance Bill** = Payable after fixed period (e.g., 30 days from sight)

### How Export Bill Negotiation Works

**Scenario:** Exporter has 30-day usance bill (importer pays in 30 days, not today).

**Problem:** Exporter needs cash today, not 30 days later.

**Solution:** Exporter sells bill to bank at discount.

**Example:**
- Bill amount: USD 100,000 (payable in 30 days)
- Bank offers to buy bill at 98% = USD 98,000 (gives exporter cash today)
- Exporter gets USD 98,000 immediately
- Bank waits 30 days, collects USD 100,000 from importer
- Bank's profit = USD 2,000 (discount)

**For exporter:** Trade-off: Get cash today (USD 98K) vs. wait 30 days (USD 100K). Exporter chooses based on cash flow needs.

## How Banks Price Export-Import Finance (Gotcha Territory)

### Pre-Shipment Credit Pricing

Banks assess:
1. **Order authenticity** — Is export order real? Check NVOCC receipt, advance payment from buyer
2. **Exporter track record** — Has exporter delivered exports on time before?
3. **Commodity type** — Steel rods (stable, liquid collateral) vs. fashion jewelry (risky, hard to liquidate)
4. **Loan tenure** — Shorter = cheaper, longer = expensive

**Exam question:** "New exporter, small order, high-risk commodity. PSC rate?"  
**Answer:** Higher (all 4 factors risky). Bank charges 7-8% vs. 4-5% for established exporter with stable commodity.

### Post-Shipment Credit Pricing

Banks assess:
1. **LC backing** — Confirmed LC? (Low risk, cheap rate) vs. Unconfirmed? (Higher risk, expensive rate)
2. **Buyer's country** — Germany? (Safe, low rate) vs. Venezuela? (Risky, high rate)
3. **Tenor** — 90 days or 180 days? (Longer = expensive)
4. **Invoice size** — USD 100K (easy to move) vs. USD 1M (needs syndication, complex)

**Exam question:** "Post-shipment backed by unconfirmed LC from Ukraine. Rate?"  
**Answer:** Expensive (unconfirmed + risky country). Bank charges 6-7% vs. 2-3% for confirmed LC from Germany.

## ECGC Coverage (For Export Finance)

**ECGC** = Export Credit Guarantee Corporation (government agency, covers export credit risk)

**What it does:** Insures bank's export credit losses.

**Example:**
- Bank gives exporter post-shipment credit backed by ECGC
- Exporter defaults, cannot repay
- ECGC covers 90-95% of bank's loss
- Bank only loses 5-10% (own risk)

**Effect:** Bank charges LOWER interest for ECGC-backed credit (bank's risk is lower).

**For exporter:** ECGC-backed credit is cheaper. Exporter often chooses it.

## The Gotchas (Exam Traps)

### Gotcha 1: Pre-Shipment ≠ Unlimited

**What students think:** "Bank gives pre-shipment credit = exporter can borrow any amount"  
**What happens:** Bank gives up to 80-90% of order value. If exporter has order for USD 100K, max PSC = USD 90K (or less, depending on creditworthiness).

**Exam trap:** "Exporter has order for USD 100K. Bank approves USD 95K PSC. Can exporter borrow more?"  
**Answer:** NO (already at max 95%, or bank policy limit already hit). Exporter must use own funds or find another bank.

### Gotcha 2: Post-Shipment Amount Depends on LC Type

**Confirmed LC** = 95% of invoice value (bank is certain of payment)  
**Unconfirmed LC** = 80-90% of invoice value (bank bears some risk)  
**No LC** = 70-80% of invoice value (bank bears full country risk)

**Exam trap:** "Exporter with unconfirmed LC gets USD 100K invoice. Bank gives post-shipment credit. Amount?"  
**Answer:** Not 95%. Probably 85% = USD 85K (because unconfirmed LC is riskier).

### Gotcha 3: Duty Drawback Formula (Easy to Mess Up)

**Formula:**
```
Duty Drawback = (Duty Paid on Raw Materials / Cost of Raw Materials) × Export Invoice Value
```

**NOT:**
```
Duty Drawback = Duty Paid × Export Invoice Value
```

**Example trap:**
- Duty paid on materials: INR 10 lakhs
- Cost of materials: INR 100 lakhs
- Export invoice: INR 200 lakhs
- Correct drawback: (10/100) × 200 = INR 20 lakhs
- Wrong drawback (common mistake): 10 × 200 = INR 2000 lakhs (absurd, but students do this)

### Gotcha 4: Advance Licensing ≠ Duty Drawback

**Advance Licensing:**
- Given BEFORE import
- Exempts duty on raw materials coming in
- For exporters who will manufacture + export

**Duty Drawback:**
- Given AFTER export
- Refunds duty already paid
- For exporters who already incurred duty

**Exam trap:** "Exporter already imported materials, paid duty, now wants to avoid future duty. What to use?"  
**Answer:** Duty drawback (already past the import stage). NOT advance licensing (which is for future imports).

### Gotcha 5: FUBD vs. PCFC (Variants Exam Tests)

**FUBD** = Foreign Currency Unsecured Bill Discounting (post-shipment, no security needed beyond bill)  
**PCFC** = Pre-Shipment Credit in Foreign Currency (exporter gets foreign currency loan upfront)

These are variants. Exam might ask:
- FUBD is riskier than post-shipment with LC (less security)
- PCFC is similar to PSC but in foreign currency (not rupee)

**For exam:** If question says "Bill discounting without LC," think FUBD (riskier, so higher rate).

### Gotcha 6: Exporter's Credit Limits

Banks set different credit limits for each exporter:
- **PSC Limit:** Total amount bank will lend for all pre-shipment needs (e.g., INR 1 crore)
- **Post-shipment Limit:** Total amount bank will lend for all post-shipment (e.g., INR 2 crore)

**Exam trap:** "Exporter has PSC limit of INR 1 crore. Already drawn INR 60 lakhs. Gets new order for INR 50 lakhs PSC. Can bank finance?"  
**Answer:** NO (already used 60 lakhs, only 40 lakhs left in 1 crore limit). Exporter must wait for current PSC to be repaid, OR get additional credit limit from bank.

## Quick Reference (Exam Night)

- **PSC** = Before shipment, riskier, higher rate, up to 90% of order value
- **Post-shipment** = After shipment, safer, lower rate, up to 95% of invoice value
- **Advance Licensing** = Duty exemption (before import)
- **Duty Drawback** = Duty refund (after export)
- **Export Bill** = Exporter's demand for payment (can be sold to bank at discount)
- **ECGC** = Insurance for export credit (makes it cheaper for exporter, safer for bank)
- **Confirmed LC** = Post-shipment at 95%, unconfirmed = 80-90%
- **Duty Drawback formula** = (Duty/Cost) × Export Invoice, NOT Duty × Invoice
- **FUBD** = Bill discounting without LC (risky, higher rate)
- **Credit limits** = Bank sets separate PSC/post-shipment limits per exporter
