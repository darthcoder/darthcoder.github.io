## UNIT 9: IFSC & GIFT City

### What Is IFSC?

**IFSC** = International Financial Service Centre

**Definition:** Zone (physical location in a country) where international financial transactions happen with special regulatory and tax treatment

**Why created:** To attract global financial business, reduce forex outflows, compete with Singapore/Hong Kong/London

**India's IFSCs:**
- **GIFT City** (Gujarat International Financial Tec City) — PRIMARY, largest
- **IFSCS Bandra-Kurla Complex** — Mumbai (secondary)
- **IFSC Kochi** — Cochin (emerging)

---

### GIFTCity (Main One for Exams)

**Location:** Gandhinagar, Gujarat (near Ahmedabad)

**What happens there:** International banking, forex trading, securities trading, insurance, commodity trading — all in USD/forex, not INR

**Why USD/forex, not INR?** Because it's an international zone. Transactions with overseas counterparties in forex, not domestic rupees.

---

### IFSC Characteristics (Why It Exists)

**1. Special Regulatory Regime:**
- Different from domestic banking (less stringent in some areas, more in others)
- Follows international standards (Basel III, ICCR, etc.)
- RBI has separate IFSC regulations

**2. Tax Incentives:**
- Lower corporate tax rates (15-20% vs. 30% domestic)
- No TDS (tax deducted at source) on interest, dividends
- Exemption on certain fees/commissions

**3. Operating Freedom:**
- Fewer restrictions on forex positions (can take larger exposures)
- Can hold forex overnight (domestic banks have daily settlement)
- Fewer AML/KYC restrictions on certain transactions (streamlined)

**4. Forex Trading Hub:**
- Currency pairs: USD/INR, EUR/USD, etc.
- Can take larger positions than domestic banks
- 24-hour trading (connects with London, Singapore, Tokyo time zones)

---

### Who Operates in IFSC?

**IFSC Banks** = Authorized banks that setup IFSC branches  
Examples: HSBC IFSC, Standard Chartered IFSC, Bank of Baroda IFSC

**IFSC Participants:**
- Banks (setting up forex trading desks)
- Securities firms (trading forex derivatives, bonds)
- Insurance companies (reinsurance, forex hedging)
- Money changers (forex conversion)
- NBFCs (non-bank financial companies)

---

### IFSC vs. Domestic Banking (Key Differences)

| Feature | IFSC | Domestic |
|---|---|---|
| Currency | Forex (USD, EUR, GBP, etc.) | INR (rupees) |
| Customers | International (foreign banks, companies) | Domestic (Indian entities) |
| Regulation | IFSCA (International Financial Services Centres Authority) + RBI | RBI only |
| Tax Rate | Lower (15-20%) | Higher (30%+) |
| Forex Position | Can hold large overnight | Daily settlement required |
| Settlement | T+2 international (slower) | T+0 or T+1 (faster) |

---

### IFSCA (Regulatory Body)

**IFSCA** = International Financial Services Centres Authority (government agency)

**What it does:**
- Licenses IFSC banks, securities firms, insurance companies
- Sets operating standards for IFSC
- Monitors forex transactions in IFSC
- Coordinates with RBI on policy

**Exam distinction:** IFSCA regulates IFSC. RBI regulates domestic banks (indirectly IFSC too through policy).

---

### IFSC vs. Offshore Financial Centres (OFC)

**IFSC (India):**
- Onshore (in India, but in special zone)
- Regulated by IFSCA + RBI
- Part of Indian economy

**OFC (Singapore, Hong Kong, Cayman Islands):**
- Offshore (outside India)
- Not regulated by India
- Different legal system

**Difference:** IFSC is "offshore feeling" but within India, regulated by Indians.

## UNIT 10: TECHNOLOGY IN INTERNATIONAL BANKING

### What's Covered

Technology enabling international banking transactions (forex, trade finance, cross-border payments)

---

### Swift (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication)

**What:** International messaging system for banks to send payment instructions

**How it works:**
1. Bank A (India) wants to send USD 100K to Bank B (Singapore)
2. Bank A sends SWIFT message: "Send USD 100K to account XYZ at Bank B"
3. SWIFT routes message securely to Bank B
4. Bank B verifies, debits Bank A's vostro account
5. Bank B credits beneficiary's account

**Swift Codes:**
- 8 or 11 character code for each bank (e.g., HSBCINBB = HSBC India)
- SWIFT address = destination bank's SWIFT code + branch code

**Why SWIFT matters:** Universal standard. All international payments go through SWIFT. No SWIFT code = payment can't happen.

**SWIFT vs. other systems:**
- Older, slow (24-48 hours delivery typical)
- Expensive (high message charges)
- Safe (encrypted, verified)

---

### Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS)

**What:** System for instant large payment settlements in India

**How it works:**
1. Bank A submits payment instruction for INR 50 lakhs
2. RTGS system debits Bank A's account immediately
3. RTGS credits beneficiary's account immediately
4. Settlement is "gross" (not netted with other transactions)
5. "Real-time" (instant, not next-day)

**Used for:** Large inter-bank payments (typically INR 5 lakhs+ threshold)

**In international context:** RTGS is domestic. International = SWIFT.

---

### NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer)

**What:** Batch-based payment system for smaller amounts

**How it works:**
1. Payments batched (collected every 30 minutes)
2. Processed together
3. Delivery within 2-4 hours

**Used for:** Smaller payments (retail, SME level)

**Slower than RTGS** but cheaper

---

### Blockchain in Trade Finance (Emerging)

**Traditional trade finance problem:** LC documents processed manually (slow, error-prone, 5-7 days to clear)

**Blockchain solution:** Digital LC on blockchain
- Exporter submits digital docs (smart contract verifies)
- Bank verifies instantly (no manual check)
- Payment triggered automatically
- Settlement in 1-2 hours instead of 5-7 days

**Status:** Pilot phase (India, Singapore testing blockchain trade finance). Not mainstream yet.

---

### APIs (Application Programming Interfaces)

**What:** Standard way for apps to communicate with each other

**In international banking:**
- Exporter's accounting software can connect to bank via API
- Export bill submitted directly from software to bank
- Real-time status updates to exporter (no email, no calls)

**Benefit:** Faster, fewer manual errors, real-time tracking

---

### Digital Wallets & Payment Apps (International)

**For remittances:** Exporters can receive payments via PayPal, Wise, Revolut (instant conversion, cheap fees)

**Threat to SWIFT:** Much cheaper, instant settlement

**Current usage:** Limited (compliance, regulatory reasons). Growing for small remittances.

---

### CHIPS (Clearing House Interbank Payments System)

**What:** US-based, high-value payment system

**Used for:** USD transactions between major banks (like SWIFT but US-specific)

**For Indian exporters:** Payments from USA typically come via CHIPS → Bank of India's SWIFT system

## Quick Fact Dump

### IFSC (GIFTCity Main)
- **Location:** Gandhinagar, Gujarat
- **Currency:** Forex (USD, EUR, GBP, etc.)
- **Customers:** International (foreign banks, companies)
- **Regulation:** IFSCA + RBI
- **Tax:** Lower (15-20%)
- **Forex positions:** Large overnight allowed
- **Purpose:** Attract global financial business, forex trading hub

### IFSCA
- **What:** Regulator of IFSC
- **Role:** License, monitor, set standards for IFSC entities

### SWIFT
- **What:** International messaging system for bank-to-bank payments
- **How:** Banks send payment instructions via SWIFT network
- **Settlement:** 24-48 hours (slower)
- **Cost:** Expensive (charges per message)
- **Essential:** All international payments require SWIFT

### RTGS (Domestic)
- **What:** Real-time gross settlement in India
- **Speed:** Instant
- **Currency:** INR
- **Amount:** Typically INR 5 lakhs+

### NEFT (Domestic)
- **What:** Batch-based settlement (every 30 min)
- **Speed:** 2-4 hours
- **Currency:** INR
- **Amount:** Typically smaller than RTGS

### Blockchain (Emerging)
- **Use case:** Digital LCs, instant settlement
- **Status:** Pilot phase
- **Benefit:** 1-2 hours settlement vs. 5-7 days traditional

### APIs
- **What:** Standard software integration
- **Use:** Export bill submission, real-time tracking
- **Benefit:** Faster, fewer errors
