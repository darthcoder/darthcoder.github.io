## CORRESPONDENT BANKING

### Definition
Correspondent banking = Relationship between two banks (domestic and foreign) to facilitate cross-border payments and settlements.

### Nostro Account
- **What:** Our (Indian bank's) account at a foreign bank
- **Currency:** Foreign currency (USD, GBP, EUR, etc.)
- **Used for:** When we need to send money abroad
- **Example:** Bank of India opens USD account at JP Morgan
- **Risk:** Our money sitting at their bank. If JP Morgan defaults, we lose.

### Vostro Account
- **What:** Their (foreign bank's) account at our (Indian bank)
- **Currency:** INR (Indian rupees)
- **Used for:** When foreign bank needs to send money to India
- **Example:** JP Morgan opens INR account at Bank of India
- **Risk:** Their money sitting at our bank. If we default, they lose.

### Why Both Exist
- **Bilateral settlement** = Both banks maintain accounts at each other
- **Speed** = Funds already sitting at counterpart bank
- **No intermediary needed** = Direct debit/credit between nostro and vostro
- **Efficiency** = Daily batches instead of message-by-message

### Settlement Process
1. Indian exporter receives USD payment instruction from foreign buyer
2. Foreign buyer's bank (JP Morgan) debits buyer's account
3. JP Morgan debits their vostro account at Bank of India (reduces their INR balance)
4. Bank of India credits Indian exporter's USD account
5. Back-and-forth credits/debits on nostro/vostro accounts daily

## NRI ACCOUNTS

### Who Qualifies (NRI Definition)
- Indian citizen living abroad
- Resident of India but earning outside India
- Foreign national employed in India (some rules apply)
- NOT applicable to PIOs (Person of Indian Origin) in certain contexts

### Account Types & Rules

#### 1. NRE Account (Non-Resident External)
**What:** Account for foreign currency earnings of NRI  
**Eligible Funds:** Salary earned abroad, investments abroad, remittances from abroad  
**Currency:** Foreign currency (USD, GBP, EUR, etc.)  
**Repatriation:** **FREELY REPATRIABLE** (can send money out of India anytime, no limit)  
**Interest:** Paid in foreign currency  
**Tax:** TDS applicable as per applicable rates

#### 2. NRO Account (Non-Resident Ordinary)
**What:** Account for rupee earnings/income sourced in India  
**Eligible Funds:** Salary earned in India, rental income, pension, dividends from Indian companies  
**Currency:** INR only  
**Repatriation:** **RESTRICTED** (can remit only up to USD 1 million per FY, or as RBI prescribes)  
**Interest:** Paid in rupees  
**Tax:** TDS applicable (often at higher rate than NRE)

#### 3. FCNR Account (Foreign Currency Non-Resident)
**What:** Fixed deposit account in foreign currency for NRI  
**Eligible Funds:** Foreign currency brought into India  
**Currency:** Foreign currency (USD, GBP, EUR, JPY, AUD, CAD, SGD)  
**Tenor:** Minimum 1 year to maximum 5 years  
**Repatriation:** **FREELY REPATRIABLE** (like NRE)  
**Interest:** Paid in foreign currency, rate fixed at opening  
**Rate:** Linked to LIBOR (or benchmark) + bank spread, set quarterly

## Key Distinctions (Exam Pattern)

| Feature | NRE | NRO | FCNR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earnings Type | Foreign | Indian |Foreign |
| Currency | Foreign | INR | Foreign |
| Repatriation | Unlimited | Limited (USD 1M/FY) | Unlimited |
| Use Case | Expat with overseas salary | Expat with India income | Expat wants fixed return |
| Interest Rate | LIBOR-linked (lower) | Domestic rate (higher) | LIBOR-linked (fixed at opening) |

## Regulatory Rules (Important)

### NRE & FCNR (Freely Repatriable)
- Can remit principal + interest anytime, no approval needed
- No limit on remittance amount
- "Freely repatriable" = NRI's right

### NRO (Restricted Repatriation)
- Can remit up to **USD 1 million per FY** (April-March)
- OR remit entire balance on return to India (become resident again)
- Interest earned in India can be remitted but subject to USD 1M limit
- "Restricted" = RBI maintains control over rupee outflows

### Why This Distinction?
- NRE/FCNR = Foreign currency brought in → can take out freely (NRI's own money)
- NRO = Rupee earnings in India → RBI wants to control outflows (national interest)

---

## Common Exam Traps

1. **NRE vs NRO:** 
   - "Salary earned in India" = NRO (can remit max USD 1M/FY)
   - "Salary earned abroad" = NRE (can remit unlimited)

2. **Repatriation Limit:**
   - USD 1M is per FY, per NRI
   - NOT per account (if NRI has 2 NRO accounts, still USD 1M total)

3. **FCNR vs NRE:**
   - FCNR = Fixed deposit (locked tenure, fixed rate)
   - NRE = Savings/Current account (liquid, variable rate)
   - Both freely repatriable

4. **Interest Rate Direction:**
   - NRE: Lower (LIBOR-linked, global rates)
   - NRO: Higher (domestic rates, RBI controlled)
   - FCNR: Locked at opening (benefits NRI if rates fall)

## Quick Facts Dump

- **Correspondent** = Bilateral bank relationship (nostro + vostro)
- **Nostro** = Our account at their bank (assets)
- **Vostro** = Their account at our bank (liabilities)
- **NRE** = Foreign currency + unlimited repatriation
- **NRO** = Rupee earnings + USD 1M/FY repatriation
- **FCNR** = Fixed deposit in foreign currency + unlimited repatriation
- **NRI becomes resident again** = NRO can be fully remitted (special rule)
