## Part 1: What Is Risk (The Real Version)

```
No risk, no gain.
More risk = more potential gain = more potential loss.
Banking = taking risk on behalf of depositors who cannot manage it themselves.
```

### The Three Moves

| Move | What it does | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Accept** | Take the risk, earn the premium | You're exposed |
| **Hedge** | Transfer the risk to someone else | Pay a fee / give up upside |
| **Diversify** | Spread risk so one loss doesn't kill you | Reduces but never eliminates |

> **Key insight:** Hedging and diversification don't destroy risk. They **relocate** it.  
> Someone always holds the bag. In banking, the question is just: *who?*

### Risk Taxonomy (BFM Relevant)

| Risk Type | Simple Version |
|---|---|
| **Credit Risk** | Borrower doesn't pay back |
| **Market Risk** | Prices move against you |
| **Interest Rate Risk** | Rates move, your margins shrink |
| **Liquidity Risk** | You're solvent but can't pay today |
| **Operational Risk** | Internal failure — people, process, systems |

> These are not separate worlds. A credit event triggers market risk triggers liquidity risk.  
> The exam treats them as separate. Real life doesn't.

## Part 2: How CAIIB Answers Risk Questions (The Correct Version)

### The IIBF Worldview

IIBF trains compliance officers. The exam is not testing your understanding of risk.  
It is testing whether you will **say the right thing in a regulatory audit.**

```
The bank is always prudent.
The regulator is always wise.
Risk is always "identified, measured, monitored and controlled."
The customer is always "informed."
```

### The Four-Word Answer to Every Risk Question

```
IDENTIFY → MEASURE → MONITOR → CONTROL
```

If a question asks "what should a bank do about X risk?" — the answer walks through these four steps in order. The exam will never say "ignore it" or "accept it and move on."

### Elimination Rules for Risk MCQs

```
1. If an option says the bank should IGNORE a risk → eliminate
2. If an option says risk can be FULLY ELIMINATED → eliminate
3. If an option mentions PROFIT as the primary driver → eliminate
4. If two options look the same, pick the one with MORE OVERSIGHT
5. "Residual risk" always EXISTS after mitigation — never zero
```

### The Hedging Trap

Exam will offer options like:
- *"Hedge all risks completely"*
- *"Maintain adequate hedging within risk appetite"*

Always pick the second. **Complete hedging doesn't exist** and IIBF knows it.

### The Diversification Trap

```
Diversification reduces UNSYSTEMATIC risk.
It does NOT reduce SYSTEMATIC (market-wide) risk.
```

If the question mentions a market crash, recession, or RBI rate hike —  
diversification is **not the answer.** Capital buffers and liquidity are.

## The Master Meta-Heuristic (Carry This Into All of Module B)

```
Q: What is the bank doing?
→ Managing risk within its approved risk appetite framework.

Q: What should the bank do when risk increases?
→ Increase capital / provisions / oversight. Never "accept and move on."

Q: Who is ultimately responsible for risk management?
→ The Board. Always the Board. Not the CRO, not RBI, not the branch manager.

Q: What is the goal of risk management?
→ "Optimise risk-return" — not eliminate risk, not maximise profit.
```
