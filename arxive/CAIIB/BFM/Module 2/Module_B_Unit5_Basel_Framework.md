# Module B — Unit 5: Basel Framework & Capital Adequacy
### CAIIB BFM Heuristic Sheet

---

## Core Heuristic

```
Basel = Depositor's invisible shield.
Capital is the brake, not the accelerator.
Regulator is always wise. Bank is always responsible.
```

---

## Decision Tree

```
Q: What changed?
│
├── Capital changed → CRAR moves SAME direction as capital
│
├── RWA changed → CRAR moves OPPOSITE direction to RWA
│
└── Both changed → Do the arithmetic

Q: What should the bank DO?
│
├── CRAR too low → Raise capital OR reduce RWA
│
└── Correct answer is NEVER "lend less"
     → Always "raise capital / improve asset quality"

Q: Solvency or Liquidity question?
│
├── Solvency → CRAR, Tier 1, Tier 2
│
└── Liquidity → LCR, NSFR
```

---

## The Formula (only one you need)

```
CRAR = Capital ÷ Risk Weighted Assets (RWA)

Minimum: 8% (Basel I floor)
India (RBI): 9%
```

---

## Tier 1 vs Tier 2

| | Tier 1 | Tier 2 |
|---|---|---|
| What | Equity + Retained Earnings | Subordinated Debt + Provisions |
| When it absorbs loss | While bank is ALIVE (going concern) | While bank is DYING (gone concern) |
| Quality | Higher | Lower |
| Exam language | "Core capital" | "Supplementary capital" |

> **Rule:** T1 > T2 in quality always. Exam never contradicts this.

---

## Basel Evolution (3 lines)

| Version | What it added |
|---|---|
| Basel I | 8% CRAR floor. Blunt instrument. |
| Basel II | Risk-sensitive RWA. Not all assets equally dangerous. |
| Basel III | Liquidity buffers (LCR, NSFR) added. 2008 proved capital alone wasn't enough. |

---

## Liquidity Ratios (Basel III)

| Ratio | Full Form | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| LCR | Liquidity Coverage Ratio | Survive a 30-day stress run |
| NSFR | Net Stable Funding Ratio | Stable funding over 1 year |

> **Rule:** Both ratios must be ≥ 100%. Regulator always wants them HIGHER.

---

## Meta-Heuristic (Most Valuable)

```
When stuck between two options:

→ Pick the one where the REGULATOR IS WISE
→ Pick the one where the BANK IS RESPONSIBLE
→ Never pick the one where PROFIT MOTIVE is the reason

Banks don't hold capital because they have to.
They hold it "to protect stakeholders."
```

---

## Quick Elimination Rules

- RBI raises risk weight → CRAR **falls** → bank must **raise capital**
- Bank issues equity → CRAR **rises**
- Bank grows loan book aggressively → RWA **rises** → CRAR **falls**
- T1 ratio question → ignore T2 entirely
- "What is the purpose of Basel?" → **financial stability / depositor protection** (not profit, not growth)
