Value is the reification of social relations. The unit of social relation is the exchange. Things, as well as favours, deeds, obligations, debts etc are all the product of and manifestations of social relations. Social relations are measured in terms of exchange.

Marx begins his analysis of Capital with the commodity. He mentions exchange in the very first passage and much of his analysis centres around use-value and exchange-value and he does mention that exchange-value is inherent in the commodity.

> To discover the various uses of things is the work of history. So also is the establishment of socially-recognized standards of measure for the quantities of these useful objects.

The quantities of exchange are thus determined socially, even as Marx himself acknowledges. However, this ideal of reducing all values to labor time, while a good first approximation, doesn't take into account all possibilities. The same sweat shop in Bangladesh or some other third world country may manufacture both Nike shoes and off-brand Pike shoes. The bills of materials, the use-value, the labor employed and the machinery used are all the same, even then the value of the Nike shoes is much higher than the Pike shoes. Of course, this is not a revelation to traditional economics or even to Marx, but the point is that this difference is not explained by the theory of labor value, but is immediately understood if one looks at the idea that the exchange is the unit of social relation and that value is a social construct.

Value arises as much as it exists only because we humans exist to value things. Further, value is dependent on both the place and the time, thus, value depends upon the social conditions that prevail. The analysis of Marx was in a vulgar sense, limited to the Political Economy and since it was based on the idea of the commodity, it fails to embrace its own potentialities and the edifice built around it over the following century and a half or so is thus incomplete, because we chose to start from the wrong unit. We must do for exchange, what Marx did for commodity.

Indeed as it turns out, exchange is the basic unit even of human cognition. When we do science, physics or mathematics, we use equations. Which are just statements of exchange. That the value on the left side of the equation is equal to the value on the right side of the equation.

The notion of the exchange is the pan balance. Today we have much more sensitive instruments but the principle of exchange remains fundamentally the same. Which things are equal depends on their mass, their density, their volume and other physical, chemical, biological or even aesthetic considerations and to study exchange by providing a taxonomy and ontology of exchange relations would be an exercise in futility. We state so because, equivalence is not a well developed notion, even in mathematics, where we have to make do most of the time with the much weaker condition of isomorphism.

Value and hence relations of exchanges are fluid and variable. It might be argued that the mass of an object doesn't change, but on the microscopic level, all atoms are in flux and any object is constantly changing its constitution by interaction with the environment. Insofar as "reality" is considered, it is an open question that we can even isolate a "system" to study it apart from its "environment". We are constantly changing ourselves, our composition changes, our thoughts and beliefs and even our bodies change with the passage of time.

At the very same time, we have the same old questions that have haunted us for millenia. What happens when we die? Why do we grow old? Why is there a universe? Even where is the universe located? And so many questions that have troubled philosophers day in and day out from the dawn of civilization and perhaps even before that. Science, at the turn of the Enlightenment was supposed to deliver us from this faux pas. It was supposed to eliminate our gridlock. With evidence, with reason, with our superior mathematical tools, we were suppoed to answer all the questions. Yet we keep building larger colliders with the expectations that something will give way. However, every question we answer, only seems to lead to further questions. We might, go as far as to ask, has Science lived up to the expectations of Enlightenment. We might consider that Science is itself used to embellish results we desire, because Science only provides observations and not interpretations. In this we can see the Scientific Method applied to eugenics, vaccine related conspiracy theories, climate change denial and the decades long fight to prove that smoking tobacco doesn't cause cancer. Indeed, Science is a tool of the application of a method, that is a good way to make observations, but Science provides no way to distinguish between competing hypothesis that can both explain the observations and perverse arguments to the beauty of nature, the naturalness argument and so on and so forth must be made.

So we find ourselves at an impasse. Keynes expected workweeks to be of 15 hours, which only betrays that either he has not read or understood the premise of Marx. The reward for increased productivity is two-fold, lowered wages and increased work. After all didn't the housewives in the 1950s in the First World find out that home appliances only served to increase the amount of work to be done at home?

It is because of the focus on commodities instead of exchange, that Marx couldn't realise his dream. The revolution didn't happen because he only saw class difference. The class difference is a reality. However, in every exchange, every social relation there is - the Other. The counterparty. Since Marx ignored the Other, he couldn't have foreseen the rise of Fascism. He couldn't have foreseen that is what would happen.
