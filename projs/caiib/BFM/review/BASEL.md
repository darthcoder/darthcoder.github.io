
| Category | Key Factoid | Relevance to CAIIB BFM |
|----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Establishment & Timeline** | Established in 1974 by central bank governors of G10 countries | Historical foundation of international banking regulation |
| **Current Membership** | 45 members from 28 jurisdictions (as of 2019); expanded in 2009 and 2014 | Understanding evolving global regulatory framework |
| **Member Countries** | Argentina, Australia, Belgium, Brazil, Canada, China, EU, France, Germany, Hong Kong, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Korea, Luxembourg, Mexico, Netherlands, Russia, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Africa, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Turkey, UK, USA | Global scope of banking supervision standards |
| **Secretariat Location** | Bank for International Settlements (BIS) in Basel, Switzerland | Administrative hub for regulatory standards |
| **Nature of Authority** | Non-binding high-level principles; not a classical multilateral organization; no founding treaty | Standards are recommendatory, not mandatory; member implementation varies |
| **Primary Objective** | Enhance understanding of supervisory issues and improve quality of banking supervision worldwide | Focus on international regulatory convergence |
| **Purpose & Function** | Encourages convergence toward common approaches and standards through informal forum | Addresses capacity and information problems in global banking |
| **Major Standards & Guidelines** | International standards on capital adequacy, Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision, Concordat on cross-border banking supervision | Core frameworks tested in CAIIB BFM |
| **Basel Framework Components** | Basel I, Basel II, Basel III, with focus on LCR, NSFR, FRTB | Essential regulatory frameworks for exam preparation |
| **Pillar 1: Regulatory Capital** | Capital requirements, capital ratio, leverage ratio, Tier 1 & Tier 2 capital, credit risk (SA-CR, IRB, F-IRB, A-IRB), market risk, operational risk | Critical for understanding minimum capital adequacy standards |
| **Pillar 2: Supervisory Review** | Economic capital, liquidity risk, legal risk; supervisory oversight of capital adequacy | Assessment beyond regulatory minimums |
| **Pillar 3: Market Disclosure** | Transparency and disclosure requirements | Promotes market discipline through information availability |
| **Credit Risk Approaches** | Standardized Approach (SA-CR) and Internal Ratings-Based (IRB) approaches | Different methodologies for credit risk measurement |
| **Operational Risk Approaches** | Basic, Standardized, and Advanced Measurement Approaches (AMA) | Risk measurement methodologies for non-credit risks |
| **Liquidity Standards** | LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio) and NSFR (Net Stable Funding Ratio) | Key post-2008 reforms ensuring liquidity management |
| **Key Subgroups** | Standards Implementation Group, Policy Development Group, Accounting Experts Group | Oversight structure for standards development and monitoring |
| **Current Chair** | Erik Thedéen (Sweden) since May 2024 | Leadership of regulatory framework development |
| **Secretary General** | Neil Esho (Australia) since 2022; Ben Gully (Canada) starting Aug 1, 2026 | Administrative leadership of BCBS operations |
| **Related Organizations** | Joint Forum with International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) and International Association of Insurance Supervisors (IIAS) | Coordination across financial regulatory sectors |
| **Implementation Mechanism** | Members expected but not obliged to implement through domestic regulation | Regulatory adaptation at national level |
| **Governance Reporting** | Reports to G10 central bank governors; cannot communicate outside BIS without their approval | Oversight and coordination mechanism |
