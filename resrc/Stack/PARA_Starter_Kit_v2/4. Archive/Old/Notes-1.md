Value is the reification of social relations. The unit of social relation is the exchange. Things, as well as favours, deeds, obligations, debts etc. are all the product of and manifestations of social relations. Social relations are measured in terms of exchange.

Marx begins his analysis of Capital with the commodity. He mentions exchange in the very first passage and much of his analysis centers around use-value and exchange-value and he does mention that exchange-value is inherent in the commodity.

> To discover the various uses of things is the work of history. So also is the establishment of socially-recognized standards of measure for the quantities of these useful objects.

The quantities of exchange are thus determined socially, even as Marx himself acknowledges. However, this ideal of reducing all values to labor time, while a good first approximation, doesn't take into account all possibilities. The same sweat shop in Bangladesh or some other third world country may manufacture both Nike shoes and off-brand Pike shoes. The bills of materials, the use-value, the labor employed and the machinery used are all the same, even then the value of the Nike shoes is much higher than the Pike shoes. Of course, this is not a revelation to traditional economics or even to Marx, but the point is that this difference is not explained by the theory of labor value, but is immediately understood if one looks at the idea that the exchange is the unit of social relation and that value is a social construct.

Value arises as much as it exists only because we humans exist to value things. Further, value is dependent on both the place and the time, thus, value depends upon the social conditions that prevail. The analysis of Marx was in a vulgar sense, limited to the Political Economy and since it was based on the idea of the commodity, it fails to embrace its own potentialities and the edifice built around it over the following century and a half or so is thus incomplete, because we chose to start from the wrong unit. We must do for exchange, what Marx did for the commodity.

Indeed as it turns out, exchange is the basic unit even of human cognition. When we do science, e.g physics or mathematics, we use equations. Which are just statements of exchange. That the value on the left side of the equation is equal to the value on the right side of the equation.

The notion of the exchange is the pan balance. Today we have much more sensitive instruments but the principle of exchange remains fundamentally the same. Which things are equal depends on their mass, their density, their volume and other physical, chemical, biological or even aesthetic considerations and to study exchange by providing a taxonomy and ontology of exchange relations would be an exercise in futility. We state so because, equivalence is not a well developed notion, even in mathematics, where we have to make do most of the time with the much weaker condition of isomorphism.

Value and hence relations of exchanges are fluid and variable. It might be argued that the mass of an object doesn't change, but on the microscopic level, all atoms are in flux and any object is constantly changing its constitution by interaction with the environment. Insofar as "reality" is considered, it is an open question that we can even isolate a "system" to study it apart from its "environment". We are constanly changing ourselves, our composition changes, our thoughts and beliefs and even our bodies change with the passage of time.

At the very same time, we have the same old questions that have haunted us for millenia. What happens when we die? Why do we grow old? Why is there a universe? Even where is the universe located? And so many questions that have troubled philosophers day in and day out from the dawn of civilisation and perhaps even before that. Science, at the turn of the Enlightenment was supposed to deliver us from this faux pas. It was supposed to eliminate our gridlock. With evidence, with reason, with our superior mathematical tools, we were supposed to answer all the questions. Yet we keep building larger colliders with the expectation that something will give way. However, every question we answer, only seems to lead to further questions. We might, go as far as to ask, has Science lived up to the expectations of Enlightenment? We might consider, that Science is itself used to embellish results we desire, because Science only provides observations and not interpretations. In this we can see the Scientific Method applied to eugenics, vaccine related conspiracy theories, climate change denial and the decades long fight to prove that smoking tobacco doesn't cause cancer. Indeed Science is a tool of the application of a method, that is a good way to make observations, but Science provides no way to distinguish between competing hypothesis that can both explain the observations and perverse arguments to the beauty of nature, the naturalness argument and so on and so forth must be made.

So we find ourselves at an impasse. Keynes expected workweeks to be of 15 hours, which only betrays that either he has not read or understood the premise of Marx. The reward for increased productivity is two-fold, lower wages and increased work. After all didn't the housewives in the 1950s in the First World find out that home appliances only served to increase the amount of work to be done at home.

Are we claiming that value doesn't arise from congealed labor? Value is the reification of social relations and labor is just one component of it. Labor and capital are both important for the production of commodities. And in the case of financial capital where the circuit reduces to $M \rightarrow M^*$ where $M^*$ is a certain value of capital which is usually different from $M$. Ideally, for the Capitalist, $M^*$ should be greater than $M$ (otherwise there is no point in the investment) but depending on factors beyond the Capitalists control(often classified as risk to group together a great number of factors) it might end up being lesser than $M$.

For the capitalist, then it is surely a matter of preference whether to invest in commodities or in any of the wide variety of Capital Markets of different kinds whether they be Securities, Debt, Derivatives, Equities and so on and so forth. In fact, Marx couldn't really have foreseen the extent of financialization of Capital and its globalisation and the rich tapestry of tax havens that spread like a spider's web engulfing the world because the technology was simply not there.

In the time of Marx, there were only Mechanical machines that required an operator to operate them, robots and automation had to wait another hundred years to be born. This is often cited as a weak point of Marxist analysis and the fact that there are fully autonomous factories that don't have workers and hence the value created by them cannot be congealed human labor because human labor was simply not involved. We have the following counterargument for that. Mechanical machines acted as a multiplier for physical human labor. A single worker could be made more productive and reduce the effort in the form of "socially necessary human labor" to produce something, making it cheaper. Similarly, automation acts as a repository of human labor. The basis of the robot is the algorithm and the procedure. The robot can autonomously perform stored behavior. Thus, it is only human labor but once removed. 

Now with the Age of the Fourth Industrial Revolution upon us, where it is very difficult to separate fact from fiction (and look at the situation in an hypeless objective manner) we now have deep learning capable robots that can even in a sense store cognition, thus we seem to be in a way, making robots more capable passing more and more of our human essence into the machines. This makes our machines more like humans, while humans become in essence machines with lesser and lesser possibility of deviation from "Standard Operating Procedures" and reducing the scope of human creativity, ingenuity and intuition in the process of production. 

Our claim is simple, machines worked as labor multipliers, earlier generations of robots worked as repositories of stored human labor which allowed them to perform pre-stored manoeuvres and perform repetitive actions and now robots can begin to take decisions based on the deep stores of data that their AI acts on. These decision criterias still need to be programmed into the machine. Thus the process of production is becoming more and more a cognitive endeavour from being a purely physical endeavour.

Of course, questions of what is intelligence, what is cognition and what is consciousness and what to make of the machines and what to make of the hype around it all are pertinent and we will have to look at all of them in order to reach a satisfactory analysis of Capital. But one thing is sure, without Capital, there never would have been the need for any of the stages of the Industrial Revolutions.

One more fact that jumps out is that the programmers and engineers that design the machines and robots are paid more handsomely than the workers who perform the actual work. This is in a longstanding trend that has existed since the Dawn of the Industrial Era. Cognitive labor is valued more than Physical Labor and that is just a consequence of the social relations. The fundamental difference between human labor and machine labor is that human labor is malleable, the same human can perform tailoring, sewing, weaving, metallurgy, casting, agriculture or whatever, but machines are limited in the range of the activities they can perform and most machines can perform one or two tasks. Even general purpose computers, of which the Turing Machine in the ideal form, even though it can perform any calculation, can only be made to operate on one calculation at a time on one chip and that must be programmed into it. A general purpose computer that computes itself is like a self-spelling wand that performs spells of its own volition, thereby making the Wizard superfluous.

> When we do science, e.g physics or mathematics, we use equations.

To briefly return to this earlier point, in so far as Science has been "successful" and Philosophy hasn't its because of the reliance of Science on the toolbox of the equation. Science is about measurement and equating things. This gives it a veneer of objectivity that Philosophy lacks. Of course we tried to find the same robes for Philosophy in the form of Analytic Philosophy, however it is not a good fit for the methods of Philosophy. Science cannot compare apples with oranges, but the whole purpose of Philosophy is to compare apples with oranges to look between different things and try to find constructions, deconstructions, territorialisations and deterritorializations and to reconstruct in terms of different concepts the same few ideas. In such a manner, it can be claimed that Philosophy cannot solve anything as it can only ever answer a question with a new question. Much of Analytic Philosophy is ontic and the whole purpose seems to debase into the construction of Categories and Ontologies. We keep taxonomizing concepts into arbitrary sets of elements and then we discard the old taxonomy and come up with a new one.

Is the solution merely to introduce equations into Philosophy, can Philosophy even claim to have a method? Feyerabend would say that even Science doesn't have a method and what do we truly have apart from falsification? Computing machines arose as a means to mechanise the process of producing proofs. In this mathematical utopia, the mathematician would just feed a set of axioms into the machine and it would spit out all possible theorems and their proofs. However, since God hates us all (and me personally), that was not to be Godel showed that this was to remain a pipe dream forever. However, the pipe dream led to the development of computers as we know them, so we can call each other names on Social Media.

Social media mediates social relation and it is now the primary medium of social interaction for most people. It creates value and then it absorbs it, feeding crumbs of it to its workers (or rather slaves since we are feeding the algorithm for free). Make no mistake, it is possible to get horribly rich on Social Media. However, this social production, which is now far surpassing whatever commodities were produced in earlier eras, is now the true production of our age. However, again, Marx couldn't have anticipated this. AI can write and draw and make movies too. Does this mean we have achieved the Capital's wet dream of removing labor from the process of production? Yes and no. It still requires the prompt and the process of creation is still manual. But that just kicks the can further down the road. Can the capitalist, with further refinement just automate the whole pipeline? What even is the difference in the SEO'ed articles that litter the web and the fevered hallucinations that ChatGPT spits out, a simulacra of human creation is it not? The problem again, becomes of production. If the Capitalist can automate the whole production of social media and indeed all media, there are no limits to its production, save how much compute the Capitalist can throw at the problem. This would only serve to flood the market, already YouTube gets millions of hours of programming every day which is never really seen. We would suddenly have trillions and quadrillions of hours of programming every second. But the attention span is limited. In this attention economy, the product can be unconstrained, but the consumer is limited. Would we then have AI watching the shows and the ads and spending money and clicking links? Would one Capitalist's AI consoom all that another Capitalist's AI produces? Would that be called a market? Would that be called an economy? It seems unplausible. In this field, the only way to valorize capital is to appropriate small amounts of it from a large number of the underclass. Otherwise there is no meaning to Capital and no scope for its growth. The poor must have money, if only to spend on the goods (be they social, cognitive or material). This leads us to our first claim, which we will take as an axiom.

> #Axiom 1:
>
> Money exists only in circulation.

Money at rest or in a vault might as well not exist. Money is purely kinetic and cannot exist out of circulation. This brings us to the idea of "the Promise". Arguably the most fundamental of all social relations. The primary social relation is the promise. It underpins all of the political-theologico-socio-economic fabric that holds us together. The fiction of the society and every component and grouping of it is held together by the promise. We will look at it later in more detail.

## The Premise of the Social Economy

The term Attention Economy has gained much credence in the recent years as more and more of our time has been spent on Social media consuming "content", according to a very prescient commentator on HackerNews, (to paraphrase) Content is the void that fills the space between the adverts. In the time of Marx, the Economy revolved around commodities. Even today commodities hold a position of extreme importance in our society and for the individuals (to remind ourselves of Thatcher's quote: There are no societies, only individuals). However, the position is arguably not as central as it used to be. The central component of most societies today is the Service sector and arguably it only produces an intangible social product - the modification of social relations on the basis of financial capital. In this we refer to Capital not in the Marxian sense, but in the sense it is employed by students and professionals of finance - capital then is a sum of money. All of finance is then, just a process of its allocation, the fact the people either have a lot of it and want small returns from it periodically or the fact that people get small amounts periodically and want to invest a large sum in one go. This can be for tangible needs like housing or intangible like a college degree. In fact based simply on price, the cost of housing and college are in the same ball park as is the cost of many other things like weddings, medical bills and local election campaigns. Thus capital is increasingly being deployed to meditate social relations (try getting healthcare with insurance e.g.).

The economy and its most productive sectors (viz services and finance) are involved in moulding and modifying social relations instead of producing tangible goods or transforming sums of money. In most cases both these activities need to be performed at the same time and one can help the cause of another. We can see that the role that machines performed for human labor, is performed by algorithms for cognitive labor expended in social relations. Social relations are getting more and more financialized as a result of this transformation. The process of loans needs to be streamlined and a multitude of innovations like "Credit Scores", "Fraud Check", "Customer De-duplication" etc that had to be performed manually now need to be done with the help of algorithms. This is but the mechanisation of cognitive labor. Giving loans and accepting deposits is not the issue, Banks can easily come up with guidelines that can allow for full automation of the process of giving credit and accepting deposits. Many neo-banks, e.g. don't have physical branches and the whole field of fintech is coasting on these perceived assumptions.

To now turn our attention to a most pertinent matter, when tangible goods are exchanged, we can see the exchange of value, in services and in finance we can still spot the congelation of human labor and the exchange of value, but what about the social economy? Sure the content is made with human labor, but the exchange is for free. Attention is the currency of this new economy, which allows economies of scale to operate on social relations. Before the dawn of social media, social relations were either mass media and uninteractive or were interactive but of a limited scale. Social media allows the scaling up of social relations to let them remain interactive while being on an unprecedented scale. The Feed, the Homepage's bastard child itself evokes imagery of consumption. You go to the Feed to well, feed and consume. The transition from Capital to Cloud Capital - the thesis of Techno-Feudalism by Varroufakis, means that instead of production, the Cloud Capitalists are now a rentier class, extracting profits by mediating access to the platforms of production and consumption of content. When we consume a physical good, it disappears, becomes unusable, becomes garbage - refuse. But content can be reconsumed, "forked", "remixed", recreated and renewed. The unit of the social exchange is the meme, not in the sense that CuLtURaL ChrIsTiAn Mister Richard Dawkins intended, but in the way it is utilised on the internet. 

However attention is ever scarce and fickle. A lot of cognitive labor is deployed into streamlining the process of delivering content interspersed with ads of course, with the goal of keeping the consumer ensnared and get them to click the links, to make the purchases. However, all of this is mediated by the Algorithm.

### The Algorithm

With AI, the old late Cold War era anxiety about the destruction of humanity by machines came back to the fore. AI can launch our nuclear codes. Once it becomes "as intelligent as a human", the claim goes, it can recursively self-improve itself and become a Silicon God and once it becomes a God, it can toy with us humans. It can make us our slaves and extract us for resources like the Matrix, or just outright exterminate us ala Terminator and other similar works of fiction. However, the truth is already stranger than fiction. As Cory Doctorow put it, "the internet is just four websites filled with screenshots of the other three." The internet is not the Wild Utopian Frontier land it used to be. We interact with the whole of the universe mediated by the internet and what we see on the internet is just what the Algorithm reveals. No one quite grasps how the Algorithm works. Not even the people who made or maintain it.
