# How do you prioritize your tasks when everything feels urgent?

> You know what they say, when everything is urgent - nothing is urgent.
  To get to the bottom of this, try to ask yourself - what would happen if I don't complete this task (on time), what would the consequences be? Then assign the highest priority to the tasks with the most major consequences, and delay or delegate the tasks with the smallest consequences.

> Eisenhower matrix. Basically divide it further.

> First of all, don´t panic. When I am in that situation I go what I call my compass which consists of my 5 most important values. This gives me a first orientation. Then I try to delegate. If that´s not possible, make a plan and start working as efficiently as you can. Although if you got into such a situation in the first place, it is likely that there is some flaw in your planning skills. Find out what that is, so you can prevent in the future. Accept the failure, when it occurs, try not to get there again

# My mind is a mess and I have no organizational system - what do you recommend?

> Step 1 is always capture - whether pen and paper, app, etc. Get everything down when presented, or soon after. Don't over plan/schedule. Hit the broad strokes. Any surprises that will occur can be dealt with. Get the right attitude. If you believe you're "a mess", you will be. Try to believe that you can handle it. I use GTD philosophy when overrun. Get small tasks checked off and it gives a boost to confidence.
> 
> As to paper or apps, I use Google tasks. It's free, has a short learning curve and integrates to the calendar with notifications. Obsidian is good for building a knowledge base to support you that you can have on mobile for ease of use.
> 
> For stress, try gardening. Even taking a few minutes with a pot plant, like pothos, helps relieve anxiety.
> 
> Lastly, I encourage self talk, out loud. Getting your thoughts audible can clarify things and help you make decisions which are well reasoned.

> I've tried a couple different things. All are pretty time-consuming, but what's been most helpful is taking it in steps:
> 
> 1. Braindump
>     
> 
> - Set a timer for like 10 mins and just write down as many things u can remember as possible that are on your mind. Having this in list format is helpful (ie. like a master list in a google doc)
>     
> 
> 1. Organize the list
>     
> 
> - This is the hard part (at least for me). Go through each task and move it into a category (eg. admin, work, health, etc..). This helps it feel a bit less overwhelming and gives some structure to your tasks.
>     
> 
> 1. Prioritize each task
>     
> 
> - Go through each category & highlight the tasks that are the most important/urgent for you to do. Whatever is left over will be backlogged for another time. You can go back to these backlogged tasks the next time you plan your day & go through your prioritization exercise again.
>     
> 
> 1. Time box each task
>     
> 
> - Whatever has been highlighted as the most important/urgent thing to do for the day should be timeboxed and put into your day plan. Think about the task and try to estimate how much time it'll take you (I often leave some extra room in case things take me longer than i think).
>     
> 
> 1. Schedule it in
>     
> 
> - Once you've timeboxed, now schedule it into your calendar so you have accountability. I often will add some reminders an hour before the next task to prompt me to finish up whatever I'm doing on time.

# How to make a daily routine?

> I found that dividing my day into three sections, such as (morning routine - midday routine - evening routine) is efficient.
> 
> For example here is my routine:
> 
> **Morning routine**
> 
> - Wake up at 8:00AM
>     
> - Drink a one liter of water
>     
> - Get some exercise
>     
> - Take a Shower
>     
> - Start Working
>     
> 
> **Mid-day routine**
> 
> - Tidy up
>     
> - Take 30 mins nap
>     
> - Continue working
>     
> 
> **Evening routine**
> 
> - Take a walk after dinner
>     
> - Brush my teeth
>     
> - Prepare for the next day
>     
> - Read a book
>     
> 
> You can start with one habit in each section. Do a self reflection to understand yourself better And modify your system until you feel a kind of stability in your day. It may takes time until you feel satisfied with your day. Continue learning from your mistakes and constantly adjusting.

> Start from waking at the same time everyday

> Firstly, a strict routine is quite overrated. It matters what you do, but the exact time or being religious about the sequence doesn't matter as much. If you wake up at different times with plenty of sleep, you're already way ahead of people who don't get enough but have a tight routine!
> 
> I try to keep things as flexible as possible.
> 
> I do have a few things for my morning routine. Hydration is crucial there, so I drink 1L of water as soon as possible. I try to get myself energized (usually music but things like some light exercise can work). I go through my main priorities for the day (I write them out at night) and then I start with the simplest thing I can do for them.
> 
> That said:
> 
> You've got to know yourself. Some people need more structure in their lives where others are more unstructured/chaotic. Depending on who you are, you might look for more or less structure in your routine.
> 
> On the chaotic end, I just write down 3-5 must-do activities. I do those at some point in my day, whatever fits my schedule. Others will tell you the more structured way.

# I’ve Felt Stuck Since 2020, and It’s Driving Me Nuts—Does Anyone Else Relate?

> You’re going to have to get out of your comfort zone

> The way you get out of a downward spiral is to soft falling. Don’t worry about climbing up yet. Just worry about feeling better.
> 
> - Do a little exercise each day
>     
> - Eat a little bit better
>     
> - Find some alternatives to replace consuming. (Reading, going for a walk, learning something you are interested in, talk to a family member or a neighbor, etc)
>     
> - Get your sleep schedule on track. Melatonin was a life saver for me since I suck at sleeping.
>     
> - Find something that you want to work towards and start working towards it. Find others with the same goal and connect. Join groups, local communities, etc. Maybe you want to get fit. Maybe you want to make more money. Maybe you want to find more friends. Whatever you do, find something you WANT to do.

# Why I can't do anything?

> According to Ali Abdaal, there are three core reasons for procrastination:
> 
> - Uncertainty
>     
> - Fear
>     
> - Inertia
>     
> 
> Solutions
> 
> - Uncertainty: define the outcome of the task or project you want to complete, define the very first step you need to take
>     
> - Fear: label the emotion you're feeling and ask yourself how confident you actually _need_ to be (this gets your brain out of the amygdala and into the frontal lobe)
>     
> - Inertia: but momentum with small wins. Set a timer for 5 minutes and commit to working on anything for that time then give yourself full permission to stop after the timer. Log your wins each day.
