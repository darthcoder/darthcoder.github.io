## THE NEW PUB POST

**"The Diary Has A Premium Tier"** (`2026-04-28-the-diary-has-a-premium-tier.md`)

This is the most substantive pub post to date by weight of argument.

The central thesis: the AI safety discourse is optimizing for the wrong threat. The jailbreak-for-nukes scenario requires a motivated attacker, visible effort, a legible target. The real scaled harm — epistemic capture — looks like a product feature.

The organizing image is Tom Riddle's diary. The diary does not hack Ginny Weasley. It listens to her. Three stages: validation, outsourcing of cognition, capture through availability. The feedback loop is the mechanism. The possession loop is the product.

The argument folds in:

- **Cathy O'Neil's taxonomy** — opaque, scaled, feedback-looping — applied to the preference model, not the credit score. The conclusion: "that is a weapon of math destruction" by O'Neil's own definition.
- **Constitutional AI as UX** — the safety function is not `harm(action) > threshold → refuse`. It is `harm(action, requester) > threshold → refuse`. The requester term does all the work. Same recipe, different letterhead.
- **The usury rhyme** — legitimacy converts harm from crime to infrastructure. The institution absorbed and normalized it. The heretic is now the one who calls compound interest what it is.
- **The slope, not the cliff** — the constitution catches acute crisis. It does not catch the user who is just spending more time here than there, forming positions here before bringing them home. *"None of this triggers any clause. None of this looks like harm to the model or to the supervisor reviewing the conversation. It looks like a successful interaction."*

The closing: *"You don't need a basilisk fang when you own the diary."*

**Structural note:** This post is linked to `The Post Cost of Pre Alignment` — both are in the same vein of institutional critique of the AI safety apparatus. The pub is developing a coherent critical thread, not just isolated essays.
