# CAIIB BFM Module A: Unit 6
## External Commercial Borrowings (ECB) & Foreign Investments in India

---

## ECB (External Commercial Borrowing)

### What Is It?

**ECB** = Loan borrowed by Indian company from foreign lender (bank, FI, investor) in foreign currency

**Who borrows:** Indian companies (manufacturing, infrastructure, IT, etc.)  
**Who lends:** Foreign banks (JP Morgan, Deutsche, HSBC), foreign DFIs (World Bank, Asian Development Bank)  
**Currency:** Foreign currency (USD, EUR, GBP, etc.)  
**Tenor:** Medium-term (3-7 years typical)  
**Rate:** LIBOR + spread (market-determined, not RBI-set)

---

### Why Companies Use ECB

**Problem:** Indian company needs large capex (factory expansion, machinery, infrastructure). Domestic banks can't lend enough.

**Solution:** Borrow from foreign lender in USD. Convert to INR, use for capex.

**Example:** Tata Steel needs USD 100M for new steel mill. Borrows from World Bank at LIBOR + 2%. Gets USD, converts to INR, builds mill.

---

### ECB Regulations (RBI-Imposed)

**Eligible Borrowers:**
- Manufacturing companies
- Infrastructure companies (power, telecom, ports)
- IT companies
- NOT eligible: Real estate, trading companies, financial intermediaries

**End Use Restrictions:**
- ALLOWED: Capex (machinery, factory, infrastructure)
- NOT ALLOWED: Working capital, acquisition of existing assets, speculative investment

**Amount Limits:**
- Large companies: Can borrow large ECBs (no hard cap, depends on creditworthiness)
- MSMEs: Separate, smaller limits

**Tenor Requirements:**
- Minimum 3 years (ECB must be long-term, not short-term)
- Short-term forex borrowing (under 3 years) = Not ECB, different rules

**All-in-Cost Ceiling:**
- RBI sets maximum all-in-cost (AIL) = LIBOR + maximum spread
- Example: If LIBOR is 2% and AIL is 4.5%, company can't borrow at LIBOR + 3% (exceeds ceiling)
- Current AIL (varies): Usually 4-5%

---

### Types of ECB

**FCCBs (Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds):**
- Bond issued in foreign currency
- Convertible to equity of Indian company
- If not converted, redeemed in foreign currency
- Risk: Company's equity gets diluted if converted

**External Commercial Loans (ECLs):**
- Straight loan (not convertible)
- Borrowed in forex, repaid in forex (company bears forex risk)

**Buyers Credit:**
- Foreign buyer's bank finances purchase of Indian goods
- Indian exporter gets paid, foreign buyer owes forex
- Indian exporter doesn't directly owe forex

---

### ECB vs. Domestic Loan (Why ECB Is Cheaper)

**Domestic bank loan:** 8-10% (rupee-denominated, RBI regulated)  
**ECB (LIBOR + spread):** 2-3% LIBOR + 1-2% spread = 3-5% (forex-denominated, market-determined)

**Why ECB is cheaper:**
- Global forex rates are lower than domestic rates
- Competition among global banks (spreads narrow)
- Indian company's forex risk (if rupee weakens, repayment in INR gets more expensive)

**Trade-off:** Lower rate, but forex risk (rupee depreciation = higher INR repayment)

---

### Debt-Equity Ratio Norm

**RBI Norm:** ECB borrowing + other external debt should not exceed 3:1 of company's equity

**Example:**
- Company equity: INR 100 crore
- Max ECB allowable: 3 × 100 = INR 300 crore

**Purpose:** RBI wants to ensure company doesn't get over-leveraged (too much debt, too little equity).

## FDI (Foreign Direct Investment)

### What Is It?

**FDI** = Foreign investor (company, PE fund, individual) invests in Indian company to gain control/stake

**Who invests:** Foreign companies (Apple setting up factory), foreign PE funds (buying stake in Indian startup)  
**What they get:** Equity stake (ownership) + control rights  
**Currency:** Forex (foreign investor brings forex in)  
**Entry:** FDI comes into India (forex inflow), boosts BoP

**Key difference from ECB:** ECB is borrowing (must repay). FDI is investment (no repayment, ownership change).

---

### FDI Regulations (FEMA Rules)

**Automatic Route (Allowed without approval):**
- Foreign investor can directly invest in most sectors (manufacturing, IT, pharma, etc.)
- No RBI approval needed if within automatic route sectors
- Registration with RBI sufficient

**Restricted Sectors (Government Approval Needed):**
- Multi-brand retail (Walmart, Amazon)
- Single-brand retail (above 51% foreign)
- Telecom
- Insurance
- Airlines
- Defense
- Atomic energy
- Media (newspaper, TV)
- Real estate
- Pension funds
- Multi-level marketing

**Prohibited Sectors:**
- Atomic energy (100% prohibited)
- Lottery business
- Gambling
- Nidhi companies (informal lending)

---

### FDI Limits (By Sector)

**Insurance:** Max 49% FDI (rest must be Indian)  
**Telecom:** Max 49% (foreign telecom company can't own majority)  
**Multi-brand retail:** Max 51% (to protect Indian retailers)  
**Single-brand retail:** Up to 100% (if through automatic route after 2021 liberalization)  
**Manufacturing:** Usually 100% FDI allowed

---

### Why Companies Attract FDI

**For Indian company:**
- Gets forex capital (doesn't need to borrow)
- Gets foreign expertise, technology, market access
- Risk: Ownership dilution (foreign investor owns stake)

**For foreign investor:**
- Access to Indian market (1.4B people)
- Lower manufacturing costs (cheap labor, land)
- Tax incentives (some sectors get tax breaks)

## ODI (Outbound Direct Investment)

### What Is It?

**ODI** = Indian company invests in foreign company/project

**Who invests:** Indian company (Infosys opening office in USA, Tata acquiring factory in UK)  
**Where invested:** Foreign country  
**Currency:** Indian rupee (converted to forex, sent abroad)

**Reverse of FDI:** India is now the investor (not investee)

---

### ODI Regulations (FEMA)

**General Rule:** Indian resident/company can invest abroad only up to prescribed limits

**Under Liberalized Remittance Scheme (LRS):** Individuals can invest abroad up to USD 250K/FY (personal investment, not business)

**For Companies:**
- Can invest abroad if:
  - Investment is in real sector (manufacturing, IT services, infrastructure)
  - Foreign direct investment (not speculative)
  - Company has net worth threshold (varies)

**Sectoral Caps:**
- Some sectors have limits (IT companies have higher limits, real estate has stricter limits)

## Quick Fact Dump

### ECB Facts
- **Eligibility:** Manufacturing, infra, IT (NOT real estate, NOT trading)
- **End use:** Capex only (NOT working capital)
- **Tenor:** Min 3 years
- **Rate:** LIBOR + spread (cheaper than domestic loans)
- **Debt-equity norm:** Max 3:1 (ECB + external debt vs. equity)
- **All-in-cost ceiling:** RBI-set max rate (currently ~4-5%)

### FDI Facts
- **Automatic route:** Most sectors (no approval)
- **Restricted:** Retail, telecom, insurance, media, airlines
- **Prohibited:** Atomic energy, lottery, gambling
- **Sector caps:** Insurance 49%, Telecom 49%, Multi-brand retail 51%, Manufacturing 100%
- **Entry:** Increases BoP (forex inflow)

### ODI Facts
- **For individuals:** Max USD 250K/FY (under LRS)
- **For companies:** Limited, sector-specific caps
- **Eligible investment:** Real sector (manufacturing, services, infrastructure)
- **Exit:** Decreases BoP (forex outflow)

## Common Exam Traps

### Trap 1: ECB End Use

**Q:** "Company borrowed ECB. Can it use for working capital?"  
**A:** NO (ECB is capex only. Working capital = different borrowing instrument)

### Trap 2: ECB Tenor

**Q:** "Company took 2-year forex loan from foreign bank. Is it ECB?"  
**A:** NO (ECB minimum tenor is 3 years. 2-year = commercial loan, different rules)

### Trap 3: FDI Sector Limits

**Q:** "Foreign company wants 60% stake in Indian insurance company. Allowed?"  
**A:** NO (Insurance sector max FDI is 49%. 60% exceeds limit)

### Trap 4: ECB vs. FDI

**Q:** "Foreign investor puts USD 10M into Indian startup. Is it ECB or FDI?"  
**A:** FDI (investor is buying equity stake, not lending). ECB = borrowing (must repay).

### Trap 5: ODI vs. FDI Direction

**Q:** "Indian IT company opens subsidiary in USA. What is this?"  
**A:** ODI (Indian company is investing abroad). FDI would be if foreign company invested in India.

### Trap 6: All-in-Cost Ceiling

**Q:** "LIBOR is 3%. RBI's AIL is 4.5%. Company wants to borrow at LIBOR + 2%. Allowed?"  
**A:** YES (LIBOR + 2% = 5%, but wait, that exceeds 4.5% AIL). NO. Not allowed (3% + 2% = 5% > 4.5% ceiling).

### Trap 7: Debt-Equity Norm Enforcement

**Q:** "Company has equity of 50 crore. Already borrowed ECB of 120 crore. Can it borrow another ECB of 40 crore?"  
**A:** NO (Current: 120 crore ECB + 40 crore = 160 crore debt. Max allowed: 3 × 50 = 150 crore. 160 > 150, exceeds limit).
