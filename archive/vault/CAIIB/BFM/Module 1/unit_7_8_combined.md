## UNIT 7: RISKS IN FOREIGN TRADE & ECGC

### The Problem (Why ECGC Exists)

Exporter ships goods to buyer in unstable country. Two risks:
1. **Political Risk** — War, coup, government action, currency blockade, expropriation
2. **Commercial Risk** — Buyer goes bankrupt, refuses payment, delays payment >6 months

Exporter cannot control these. Bank won't finance without insurance. Deal stuck.

**Solution:** ECGC insures exporter's credit risk. Bank finances with confidence.

---

### What IS ECGC?

**ECGC** = Export Credit Guarantee Corporation (government agency, 100% owned by RBI)

**What it does:** Insurance for export credit losses (political + commercial risks)

**Who uses it:** Exporters, banks (on behalf of exporters)

---

### ECGC Coverage (Key Facts)

**Coverage Amount:** 90-95% of loss (exporter/bank bears 5-10% deductible)

**Political Risks Covered:**
- War, civil war, civil unrest, strikes
- Government action (embargo, moratorium on payments, expropriation)
- Currency blockade (buyer's country prevents forex transfer)
- Non-transfer of funds (government won't allow payment out)

**Commercial Risks Covered:**
- Buyer insolvency (bankruptcy)
- Default (buyer refuses to pay)
- Non-acceptance of goods (buyer rejects after inspection)
- Protracted default (payment delayed >180 days from due date)

**Non-Covered Risks:**
- Quality disputes (buyer says goods are bad — exporter's responsibility)
- Contractual disagreement (buyer has legitimate complaint)
- Exporter's own negligence (exporter didn't ship properly)

---

### ECGC Risk Categories (Country Rating)

ECGC divides countries into 4 risk categories:

**Category A** (Lowest risk) — Germany, USA, UK, Japan  
- Premium: 0.5% - 0.75% of invoice value annually

**Category B** (Low-medium risk) — Singapore, UAE, Mexico  
- Premium: 0.75% - 1.25%

**Category C** (Medium-high risk) — Brazil, Indonesia, Philippines  
- Premium: 1.25% - 1.75%

**Category D** (Highest risk) — Pakistan, Venezuela, countries with sanctions  
- Premium: 1.75% - 2.5%+

**Pattern:** Risky country = higher premium. Exporter pays extra to be protected.

---

### ECGC Claim Process

**Timeline:**
1. Payment due date passes
2. Exporter waits 6 months (for protracted default)
3. Exporter hasn't received payment for 180+ days
4. Exporter submits claim to ECGC
5. ECGC verifies: Is claim valid? (No quality dispute? Buyer really insolvent?)
6. ECGC pays 90-95% of loss to exporter/bank

**Payable** = 120 days after due date (if payment not received by then, claim can be filed)

**Payout time:** Usually 30-60 days after claim verification

---

### When Export Finance Uses ECGC

**Scenario 1:** Bank gives post-shipment credit (exporter needs cash, LC not yet confirmed)
- Bank: "I'll finance if you get ECGC cover"
- Exporter: Gets ECGC insurance (costs 1-2% premium)
- Bank finances at lower rate (bank's risk covered)
- Result: Exporter gets cheaper financing, bank is safe

**Scenario 2:** Exporter exporting to risky country
- Exporter: "I have buyer in Venezuela (risky country)"
- Bank: "No way, I'm not financing Venezuela risk"
- Exporter: Gets ECGC cover for Venezuela
- Bank: Now financing is acceptable (90-95% risk covered)

**Exam pattern:** "PSC to Venezuela, unconfirmed LC, ECGC cover vs. without ECGC"  
**Answer:** With ECGC = cheaper rate (ECGC covers political + commercial risk)

## UNIT 8: REGULATORY FRAMEWORK (RBI, EXIM, FEMA, FEDAI)

### The Big Picture

**RBI** = Central bank, sets forex policy  
**EXIM Bank** = Development bank, finances exports/imports  
**FEMA** = Law governing all forex transactions  
**FEDAI** = Self-regulatory body, sets inter-bank forex norms

---

### FEMA (Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999)

**What:** Law governing all forex transactions in India

**Core Principle:** 
- **Residents:** Can do current account transactions (trade, travel, education) freely
- **Residents:** Cannot do capital account transactions without RBI approval (investment, borrowing abroad, property purchase)

**Examples:**
- Resident paying foreign school fees? Current account = ALLOWED (no approval)
- Resident buying property in USA? Capital account = NEEDS RBI APPROVAL
- Resident remitting gifts? Depends on amount. Under LRS (250K/FY)? Allowed. Over? Needs approval.

**Key Restrictions:**
- No resident can do forex beyond specified limits without RBI
- Current account = Liberalized (mostly)
- Capital account = Controlled (needs case-by-case approval)

**Violations:** Criminal + civil penalties, asset seizure

---

### RBI's Role in Forex (What It Actually Does)

**1. Authorized Dealers (AD Banks):**
- RBI licenses banks as "Authorized Dealers" (AD)
- Only ADs can do forex transactions with customers
- ADs must follow RBI guidelines on margins, rates, position limits

**2. Policy Setting:**
- RBI decides forex regime (floating, managed float)
- RBI sets prudential limits (how much forex risk can bank take)
- Example: Bank can have max 10% open position (unmatched forex exposure)

**3. Forex Reserves Management:**
- RBI holds India's foreign exchange (USD, EUR, gold)
- Intervenes to stabilize rupee (buys when weak, sells when strong)

**4. Balance of Payments:**
- RBI monitors forex inflows/outflows
- Uses policy to maintain healthy BoP

**5. Capital Controls:**
- RBI decides who can remit, how much, under what conditions
- LRS (250K/FY) is RBI's policy
- FEMA is RBI's enforcement tool

---

### EXIM Bank (Export-Import Bank of India)

**What:** Development Finance Institution (DFI), 100% owned by Government of India

**Purpose:** Finance India's exports and imports

**Products:**
- **Pre-shipment Credit (PSC):** Exporters borrow to finance production
- **Post-Shipment Credit:** Exporters borrow against export documents
- **Buyer Credit:** EXIM Bank lends to foreign buyer (who then pays Indian exporter)
- **Supplier Credit:** EXIM Bank lends to overseas supplier (who then ships to Indian importer)
- **Credit Enhancement:** EXIM provides partial guarantees to exporters

**Interest Rates:**
- EXIM rates are 1-2% LOWER than commercial banks (subsidized by government)
- EXIM focuses on growth, not profit maximization

**Who Gets EXIM Support:**
- Exporters (especially MSMEs, new exporters, first-time buyers)
- Companies with government backing (BHEL, SAIL, etc.)
- Strategic sectors (solar equipment, healthcare, IT services)

**Exam pattern:** "Exporter with commercial bank PSC at 5% gets EXIM offer at 3.5%"  
**Why:** EXIM is development bank, cheaper financing for exports.

---

### FEDAI (Foreign Exchange Dealers' Association of India)

**What:** Self-regulatory body of forex dealers (banks trading forex with each other)

**What It Does:**
- Sets inter-bank forex transaction norms (how banks trade with each other)
- Publishes daily forex rates (benchmark rates)
- Sets bid-ask spread limits (banks can't widen spreads beyond norms)
- Ensures dealing room standards (compliance, documentation, settlement)
- Handles disputes between banks

**FEDAI Guidelines:**
- Banks must report forex positions daily
- Banks must follow settlement procedures (T+2, SWIFT messaging)
- Banks must maintain dealing room audit trails
- Banks cannot manipulate rates (collusion banned)

**Exam patterns:**
- "Bank dealing room should follow _____ guidelines" = FEDAI
- "FEDAI publishes which rates?" = Interbank forex rates (not retail rates)
- "How often banks report positions to FEDAI?" = Daily

---

### Capital vs. Current Account (Critical Distinction)

**Current Account Transactions:**
- Trade (imports, exports)
- Travel (forex for tourism)
- Education (tuition abroad)
- Insurance, shipping, consulting fees
- Income repatriation (salaries, dividends)
- **Default:** ALLOWED (liberalized)
- **Approval:** Usually not needed (unless large/suspicious)

**Capital Account Transactions:**
- Investment abroad (buying stocks, property, FDI)
- Borrowing from abroad (ECBs)
- Loans to foreign entities
- Gifts/donations (large amounts)
- **Default:** NOT ALLOWED (unless approved)
- **Approval:** Needs RBI case-by-case decision (or falls under LRS/special scheme)

**Resident Confusion (Common Exam Trap):**
- Resident wants to send USD 10K as gift = Current account? Capital account?
- Answer: Depends on how it's classified. Gift = capital. But under LRS gifting is allowed (250K/FY).
- So: Under 250K gift to family = Allowed via LRS (not capital, special scheme).

---

### Quick Fact Reference

**FEMA:**
- Current account = Liberalized
- Capital account = Controlled
- Violators = Criminal penalties + asset seizure

**RBI:**
- Sets forex policy (floating/managed)
- Manages forex reserves (buys low, sells high to stabilize rupee)
- Licenses Authorized Dealers (banks)
- Enforces FEMA

**EXIM Bank:**
- PSC, post-shipment, buyer/supplier credit
- Rates 1-2% cheaper than commercial banks
- Government-subsidized (DFI, not commercial bank)

**FEDAI:**
- Inter-bank forex norms
- Publishes daily rates
- Dealing room compliance
- Dispute resolution between banks

**Exam Pattern:**
- "Who sets inter-bank forex rates?" = FEDAI
- "Who licenses Authorized Dealers?" = RBI
- "Who finances exports at subsidized rates?" = EXIM Bank
- "Who regulates forex transactions?" = RBI (via FEMA)

---

### The Gotchas (Exam Traps)

**Gotcha 1: RBI vs. FEDAI**
- RBI = Makes policy, regulates
- FEDAI = Implements norms, manages inter-bank transactions
- Trap: "Who sets dealing room standards?" = FEDAI (not RBI)

**Gotcha 2: EXIM Bank vs. Commercial Bank**
- EXIM = Subsidized rates (government-owned, development mandate)
- Commercial = Market rates (profit-driven)
- Trap: "Which bank is cheaper for exports?" = EXIM (usually)

**Gotcha 3: Current vs. Capital (LRS Exception)**
- Normally: Gift = capital account (needs approval)
- LRS exception: Gift up to 250K/FY = allowed (not capital, special scheme)
- Trap: "Can resident gift USD 300K?" = Normally NO (capital). But if under LRS and under 250K = YES.

**Gotcha 4: FEMA Restrictions**
- Not all forex is unrestricted
- Resident CAN remit USD 250K (LRS)
- Resident CANNOT remit for business (ineligible)
- Resident CANNOT remit INR (only forex to foreigners)
- Trap: "Can resident remit INR 10 lakhs to brother abroad?" = NO (FEMA allows forex, not INR remittance)

**Gotcha 5: Open Position Limit (RBI Rule)**
- Banks cannot take unlimited forex risk
- RBI caps open position (unmatched) at ~10% of capital
- If bank is naturally long USD, it must offset (short forward, or sell spot)
- Trap: "Bank has excess USD position. Must bank action?" = YES (close position to stay within RBI limit)
